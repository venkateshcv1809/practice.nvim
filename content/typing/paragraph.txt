The workshop was quiet except for the click of keys. Mira had spent years
believing that fluency arrived all at once, like a door swinging open. Tonight
she learned it was smaller than that: one honest hour, then another.

She opened a blank buffer and did not apologize to it. The first lines were
clumsy. The next lines were less so. She breathed, unclenched her shoulders,
and let the cursor be a walking companion rather than a judge.

By midnight the work was still unfinished, and that was the gift. Unfinished
meant she could return. Unfinished meant the craft still wanted her. She saved
the file, stood, and stretched, carrying a quiet sentence with her: progress is
the decision to continue while you are still allowed to be a beginner.

Morning would bring the same desk, the same tools, the same chance to choose
patience over panic. She would sit down again. She would type the next true
line. That was enough. That was the whole practice.
